[cue 12 — common objections intro]
Now, the most common objections you'll run into. A lot of times the gap between your number and theirs is smaller than you'd think — or they don't even have a specific number, they're just testing you. And the next two lessons are dedicated entirely to objection scripts and complex situations, so you'll get plenty more practice.

[cue 13 — "I need to think about it"]
When they say "I need to think about it," you say "absolutely, it's a big decision and I'd never want you to feel rushed. Can I ask what specifically you're weighing? I might be able to give you some more information that helps." When they want to list with a Realtor, try

[cue 14 — list with a Realtor]
that's definitely an option, and I'd never tell you not to explore it. Have you gotten an estimate on what repairs might be needed to list it? And are you in a position to wait several months for the listing process?

[cue 15 — getting other offers / send in writing]
When they say they're getting other offers, that's actually fine. "That's great, you should explore all your options. Can I ask what you've seen so far? I just want to make sure you're comparing apples to apples." If they ask you to send something in writing, say "I'm happy to do that. Before I do, can I ask you a couple of quick questions so I can make sure what I send is actually relevant to your situation?"

[cue 16 — "I'm not interested" / "how did you get my number"]
When someone flat out says "I'm not interested," try "completely understand. Before I let you go, is there a reason you responded to our outreach in the first place? Sometimes things change over time and I just want to make sure we haven't missed something." "How did you get my number?" comes up a lot. "Great question. Your property matched our buying criteria, so it came through our system. We reach out to homeowners who might benefit from a fast, simple sale. Does that describe your situation at all?"
